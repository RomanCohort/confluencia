\section{Methods}

\subsection*{Ten core modules}
Confluencia integrates ten computational modules spanning drug design, epitope prediction, pharmacokinetic simulation, and circRNA-specific functional assessment:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Drug Pipeline:} Sample-size-adaptive weighted model averaging (WMA) for binding/efficacy prediction, switching from Morgan FP+XGBoost to simpler features+Ridge for N$<$2\,000.
\item \textbf{Epitope Pipeline:} MHC binding prediction with Mamba3Lite encoding and allele-aware features (MHC-I: 1\,018 dims; MHC-II: 947 dims).
\item \textbf{RNACTM:} Six-compartment pharmacokinetic simulation (injection, LNP, endosome, cytoplasmic RNA, translated protein, clearance) via adaptive RK45 ODE solver.
\item \textbf{Immunogenicity Scoring:} Five innate immune pathway assessments (RIG-I, TLR7, TLR8, PKR, OAS/MDA5) with circRNA-specific dsRNA backbone detection.
\item \textbf{Structure Prediction:} ViennaRNA wrapper for MFE structure, dsRNA region extraction, and sequence complexity estimation. For full 3D structure prediction of therapeutic-length circRNAs ($L>500$\,nt) where $O(L^2)$ memory becomes prohibitive, Confluencia integrates TorusFold Scheme 8 (sparse pair-guided hybrid, $O(L \cdot K)$). It uses ViennaRNA circ-mode Top-$K$ candidate selection with BSJ anchor attention, plus a geometric feedback loop that recovers energy-predictor-missed pairs through ring-neighborhood search and chemical-compatibility filtering. The platform also hosts Circ-CASP, a community benchmark for circRNA 3D structure prediction with 10\,000+ training sequences, 30 hidden test structures, and a 5-metric evaluation (RMSD, BSJ-closure, bond-consistency, pair-F1, conformational-diversity).
\item \textbf{m6A Prediction:} DRACH motif screening with GC-context probability estimation (not site-level prediction).
\item \textbf{IRES Prediction:} Kozak context scoring (strong/moderate/weak) and ORF completeness check (AUG-to-stop/BSJ).
\item \textbf{miRNA Sponge Analysis:} 16 oncogenic/regulatory miRNA seed database (see Supplementary Table S10); complementarity scoring, binding energy estimation, ceRNA potential calculation.
\item \textbf{RBP Binding Prediction:} 7 RBP motif database (HuR, FMR1, TDP43, FUS, IGF2BP1, YBX1, PTBP1); structure accessibility weighting.
\item \textbf{Evolutionary Optimization:} REINFORCE-based Pareto search with circRNA-specific operations (IRES optimization, modification switching, backbone mutation); see Supplementary Table S9 for hyperparameters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Validation data hierarchy}
Confluencia validation operates at three tiers with distinct data sources and purposes:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Tier 1 (Literature experimental data):} $N{=}7$ sequences with published IFN-$\beta$ measurements (Chen et al. 2019, Zhang et al. 2016) and $N{=}4$ sequences with measured half-life (Wesselhoeft et al. 2018, Liu et al. 2023). This provides \textit{quantitative} validation of immunogenicity scores and PK predictions against real experimental measurements, not pseudo-labels.

\item \textbf{Tier 2 (circBase screening):} $N{=}50$ sequences from circBase database, stratified by GC content and length. Immunogenicity labels are \textbf{pseudo-labels} generated by the platform itself, enabling consistency verification and sensitivity analysis but \textbf{not independent validation}. This tier identifies expected trends (GC-immunogenicity correlation) and tests pipeline robustness.

\item \textbf{Tier 3 (Direction consistency):} Comparison of modification rankings ($\Psi$ vs m6A vs unmodified) with literature-reported IFN patterns. Qualitative agreement provides plausibility evidence but cannot substitute for quantitative validation.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Critical distinction:} Tier 1 uses measurements from experiments published before this work; Tier 2 uses model-generated labels to assess internal consistency. Reviewers R2+R4 correctly identified that Tier 2 alone constitutes circular validation. The literature-derived Tier 1 dataset addresses this concern by providing independent experimental ground truth.

\subsection*{RNACTM compartment simulation}
RNACTM models six compartments (injection, LNP, endosome, cytoplasmic RNA, translated protein, clearance) as a linear ODE system solved via adaptive RK45 (scipy \texttt{solve\_ivp}). Rate constants from literature: unmodified half-life 6.24\,h \citep{wesselhoeft2018}, m6A 10.8\,h \citep{chen2019}, $\Psi$ 15.0\,h \citep{liu2023}. For 5mC (5-methylcytosine) and ms2m6A (2-methylthio-N6-methyladenosine), no direct circRNA half-life measurements are available; values used (5mC: $\sim$12.5\,h, ms2m6A: $\sim$20\,h) are author estimates extrapolated from linear mRNA modification studies and should be treated as assumed values pending experimental validation. Endosomal escape 5.2\% is above the 1--5\% range reported by \citet{gilleron2013}; this value is used for scenario analysis. Sensitivity analysis with 1--5\% endosomal escape range shows half-life variation of $\pm$20\%. This is a deterministic literature-parameterized simulation, not a population PK model. Rate constants are derived from published mouse/in vitro studies. Human clinical validation is lacking. The simulation is a scenario analysis tool for comparing candidate configurations. Sensitivity: $\pm$20\% rate variation changes half-life by $\leq$8.2\%. Benchmark specifications: Intel Core i7-10700K (8 cores, 3.8 GHz), 32 GB RAM, Windows 10, Python 3.10, ViennaRNA 2.6.4. Tested on $N{=}100$ circRNA sequences spanning 100--2000 nt. RNACTM simulation: mean$=0.8{\pm}0.3$ s (range 0.4--1.6 s). Stratified by length: short ($<$500 nt): 0.5${\pm}$0.1 s; medium (500--1000 nt): 0.8${\pm}$0.2 s; long ($>$1000 nt): 1.2${\pm}$0.4 s.

\subsection*{Drug and epitope pipelines}
Drug: sample-size-adaptive weighted model averaging (WMA; inverse-RMSE weighting, sometimes called "MOE" in the codebase but distinct from neural Mixture-of-Experts with learnable gating) switches from Morgan FP+XGBoost to simpler features+Ridge for N$<$2\,000; 91K ChEMBL data across 6 targets. Sample size thresholds (80/300) are heuristic choices based on typical overfitting thresholds in cheminformatics (10-20 samples per feature), not empirically optimized for this dataset; sensitivity analysis shows R$^2$ varies by $\pm$0.03 when thresholds change by $\pm$50\%. Drug R$^2{=}0.984$ for binding on small samples ($N{<}300$) is conditional on feature selection strategy (Ridge without fingerprint features; removing Morgan FP improves R$^2$ from 0.67 to 0.95 due to reduced overfitting). ADMET screening via ADMETlab \citep{xiong2024} with PAINS \citep{baell2010}; ADMET endpoints (hERG, AMES, CYP450) apply only to the small-molecule drug payload, not to the circRNA vehicle itself. circRNA-specific toxicity is assessed via innate immune pathway scoring (RIG-I/TLR/PKR/OAS/MDA5). Epitope: Mamba3Lite \citep{gu2023} encoding + allele-aware MHC features (MHC-I: 1\,018 dims; MHC-II: 947 dims via NetMHCIIpan pseudo-sequences); 288K IEDB data, 5-fold CV. \textbf{MHC-II binding prediction is provided as an experimental feature. While MHC-I binding has been validated against IEDB benchmarks (R$^2{=}$0.82), MHC-II predictions have not been independently validated and should be interpreted with caution.} MHC binding prediction addresses adaptive immunity (T-cell-mediated antigen presentation), which is distinct from circRNA innate immunogenicity (RIG-I/TLR/PKR sensing). Both pipelines CPU-only; no GPU required.

\textbf{Flexible backend architecture:} Confluencia supports multiple backend options for each prediction module, allowing users to choose accuracy-speed trade-offs:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{MHC binding:} Local model (AUC=0.80, fast, offline) or NetMHCpan API (AUC=0.92-0.96, industry best, requires network). For high-accuracy epitope prediction, users can call \texttt{predict\_mhc(peptide, backend="netmhcpan")}.
\item \textbf{Immunogenicity:} Heuristic model (default, $\sim$85ms), ViennaRNA-enhanced with structural accessibility ($\sim$150ms), or ESM-2 embeddings (experimental, $\sim$2-5s). ViennaRNA backend adds TLR structural accessibility scoring via unpaired probability.
\item \textbf{Drug binding:} Local model (R$^2$=0.95, fast, offline) or ChEMBL API (experimental data, requires network). For validation of known compounds, users can query ChEMBL directly.
\end{itemize}
This design prioritizes \textbf{research flexibility}: default backends ensure offline availability and speed; switching to external backends provides higher accuracy when network access is available. Users can select backends based on their specific accuracy requirements and computational constraints.

\subsection*{Clinical prediction (exploratory, not validated)}
Clinical prediction (Cox regression approximation with IPS/TIDE integration) is an \textbf{exploratory feature without clinical validation}. Key limitations:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Cox approximation}: Partial-feature scoring based on published gene signatures, not a full Cox proportional hazards model trained on circRNA therapeutic data. No training cohort, cross-validation, or independent test set exists for this application.
\item \textbf{IPS/TIDE applicability}: These scores were developed for cancer immunotherapy checkpoint inhibitor response prediction (Charoentong et al., 2017; Jiang et al., 2018) in oncology patients. Their applicability to circRNA vaccine recipients, who may be healthy individuals receiving prophylactic vaccination, is unclear and unvalidated.
\item \textbf{No validation statistics}: C-index, calibration plots, and Hosmer-Lemeshow tests are not available because no clinical outcome data for circRNA therapeutics exists to validate against.
\end{itemize}
Survival prediction and adverse event risk assessment should \textbf{not} be used for clinical decision-making. This module is retained for hypothesis generation only.

\subsection*{circRNA functional assessment}
Five innate immune pathways scored from sequence motifs (RIG-I, TLR7, TLR8, PKR, OAS/MDA5). \textbf{Pathway weights:} RIG-I 0.35, TLR7 0.20, TLR8 0.15, PKR 0.30. \textbf{OAS/MDA5 note:} OAS and MDA5 share the dsRNA sensing mechanism with PKR and are \textbf{not independently weighted}, as their activation is captured implicitly through the PKR dsRNA score ($>$33bp threshold also applies to OAS/MDA5). Thus, four pathway weights sum to 1.0 while covering five biological receptors. Weights are author-informed heuristics, not empirically calibrated; $\pm$0.05 perturbation changes overall immunogenicity by $\leq$4.2\% (sensitivity analysis). \textbf{Critical biological distinction for circRNA:} circRNA is a covalently closed loop with \textbf{no 5' or 3' termini}, making canonical RIG-I blunt-end recognition (which requires a free 5'-triphosphate end) fundamentally \textbf{inapplicable}. circRNA activates RIG-I exclusively through dsRNA backbone structures formed by inverted repeat sequences within the circular backbone \citep{zhang2016}. The scoring targets these internal dsRNA regions, NOT terminal structures. TLR7 and TLR8 are scored separately (GU-rich vs AU-rich motif preferences). m6A effect on immunogenicity is context-dependent (coding region m6A $\to$ immune evasion via YTHDF2; IRES-proximal m6A $\to$ may enhance translation/immune visibility). This innate immunogenicity assessment is distinct from MHC binding prediction (adaptive immunity, T-cell-mediated). Structure via ViennaRNA \citep{lorenz2011}. When ViennaRNA is unavailable, fallback mode produces coarse estimates with \texttt{precision\_tier="coarse\_estimate"}. Users should avoid using fallback results for sequences $>$500\,nt where structure prediction accuracy significantly degrades. m6A prediction: DRACH motif screening (not site-level prediction; no cross-validation with m6A-Atlas/RMBase). IRES prediction includes Kozak context scoring (strong/moderate/weak) and ORF completeness check (AUG-to-stop/BSJ). Five functional axes: half-life/modification, immunogenicity (innate), miRNA sponge, RBP binding, translation potential. Uncertainty: half-life and immunogenicity $u{=}0.15$; miRNA/RBP/translation $u{=}0.8$, yielding effective weights of $(1{-}0.8)^2{=}0.04$.

\paragraph{dsRNA backbone structure detection (RIG-I/PKR): the ONLY mechanism for circRNA.}
\textbf{Fundamental biological constraint:} circRNA is a covalently closed loop with no 5'/3' termini. RIG-I canonical blunt-end recognition requires a free 5'-triphosphate end \citep{schlee2009}, which circRNA lacks by definition. Therefore, circRNA activates RIG-I exclusively through dsRNA backbone structures formed by inverted repeat sequences within the circular backbone \citep{zhang2016}. The scoring algorithm detects internal dsRNA regions, NOT terminal structures. \textbf{Warning:} Linear RNA contaminants (incomplete circularization products) may retain 5' ends and would trigger canonical RIG-I blunt-end recognition. These are not true circRNA and require separate consideration.

dsRNA regions are detected from ViennaRNA MFE dot-bracket notation using a stem extraction algorithm. Given structure string $S$ where $S[i] \in \{``(", ``.", ``)"\}$, dsRNA backbone length $L_{\text{dsRNA}}$ is computed as:
\begin{equation}
L_{\text{dsRNA}} = \sum_{(s,e) \in \mathcal{R}} \left( \sum_{i=s}^{e} \mathbf{1}[S[i] \neq ``."] \right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R} = \{(s,e) : S[s:e]$ is a contiguous stem region with paired-base count $\geq L_{\min}\}$. The threshold $L_{\min}=33$\,bp follows \citet{nallagatla2007} for PKR activation (RIG-I has similar dsRNA length requirements). For partial dsRNA with bulges/internal loops, the paired-base count (excluding ``.") provides the effective dsRNA length. The RIG-I activation score emphasizes \textbf{dsRNA stability} as the primary determinant:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{RIG-I}} = 0.20 \cdot f_{\text{dsRNA}} + 0.40 \cdot S_{\text{MFE}} + 0.20 \cdot f_{\text{GC}} \cdot f_{\text{dsRNA}} + 0.15 \cdot \frac{L_{\text{max-stem}}}{100} + 0.05 \cdot \frac{N_{\text{stems}}}{10}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\text{dsRNA}}$ is the fraction of paired bases in MFE structure, $S_{\text{MFE}}$ is the per-nucleotide stability score (normalized from MFE range $-0.3$ to $-1.5$\,kcal/mol/nt), $L_{\text{max-stem}}$ is the maximum contiguous stem length, and $N_{\text{stems}}$ is the count of stem regions. The 40\% weight on MFE stability reflects a key biological insight: AU-pairing forms dsRNA structure, but GC-rich dsRNA (MFE $\approx -1.3$\,kcal/mol/nt) is far more stable and immunogenic than AU-rich dsRNA (MFE $\approx -0.5$\,kcal/mol/nt). When ViennaRNA is unavailable, a fallback heuristic estimates $f_{\text{dsRNA}} \approx f_{\text{GC}} \times 1.5$ from GC-driven stem formation potential.

\paragraph{TLR7 and TLR8 motif scoring.}
TLR7 and TLR8 are scored separately due to distinct motif preferences \citep{diebold2006,gorden2008}. TLR7 prefers GU-rich ssRNA motifs:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{TLR7}} = 0.70 \times \left( 0.45 \cdot \frac{N_{\text{GU}}}{L/10 \cdot 3.0} + 0.30 \cdot \frac{N_{\text{AU-rich}}}{3.0} + 0.20 \cdot \frac{f_U}{0.5} + 0.05 \cdot \frac{L}{1000} \right)
\end{equation}
where $N_{\text{GU}}$ is count of GU-rich motifs (GUUG, GUGU, UGUU, GUCU, GUUU), $N_{\text{AU-rich}}$ is AU-rich element count, $f_U$ is uridine fraction, and $L$ is sequence length. The 0.70 factor accounts for reduced ssRNA exposure in circRNA closed-loop structure. TLR8 prefers AU-rich motifs:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{TLR8}} = 0.70 \times \left( 0.40 \cdot \frac{N_{\text{AU}}}{L/10 \cdot 3.0} + 0.35 \cdot \frac{f_U}{0.5} + 0.20 \cdot \frac{N_{\text{GUUG}}}{L/10 \cdot 2.0} + 0.05 \cdot \frac{L}{1000} \right)
\end{equation}
where $N_{\text{AU}}$ counts AU-rich motifs (AUUA, UUAU, UAUU, AUUU, UAAU). Scores are clamped to $[0,1]$.

\textbf{Normalization constants derivation:} The denominators $L/10 \times 3.0$ and $L/10 \times 2.0$ normalize motif counts by sequence length and expected motif density. For a random RNA sequence, the expected number of 4-mers is $L \times 0.25^4 \approx L/256$. For GU-rich 4-mers (5 motifs), expected count is $\approx 5 \times L/256 \approx L/50$. The constant $L/10 \times 3.0 = 0.3L$ is a heuristic upper bound reflecting that immunostimulatory sequences have motif density $\sim$10$\times$ higher than random expectation. This normalization ensures scores in $[0,1]$ range for typical therapeutic circRNA sequences.

TLR7/8 activation requires endosomal localization. The scoring assumes RNA reaches endolysosomal compartments; delivery efficiency (LNP formulation, endosomal escape) is not modeled. Secondary structure affects motif accessibility, as GU-rich motifs in single-stranded loops/bulges are more accessible than those in paired regions. RNA modifications strongly modulate TLR activation: $\Psi$ (pseudouridine) reduces TLR7/8 signaling by $\sim$90\% \citep{anderson2019}; 2'-O-methylation eliminates TLR7/8 activation; N1-methylpseudouridine (m1$\Psi$, used in mRNA vaccines) nearly abolishes TLR recognition. The current scoring applies to UNMODIFIED RNA; users should apply empirical reduction factors for modified sequences.

\textbf{Limitation:} TLR scores represent motif-based potential without delivery, modification, or structural accessibility corrections. For modified sequences ($\Psi$, m1$\Psi$), divide scores by empirical factor $\sim$10 to reflect reduced TLR activation \citep{anderson2019,kariko2005}. The $\sim$10 factor derives from: $\Psi$ reduces TLR signaling by $\sim$90\% (10-fold), m1$\Psi$ abolishes recognition (infinite factor; $\sim$10 is conservative).

\paragraph{DRACH motif m6A probability estimation.}
m6A sites are screened via the DRACH consensus motif: D$=$A/G/U, R$=$A/G, A, C, H$=$A/C/U (regex: \texttt{[AGU][AG]AC[ACU]}). The modification probability at each DRACH site is estimated from local GC context:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{m6A}} = \text{clip}\left( P_{\text{base}} + f_{\text{GC}}^{\text{local}} \times 0.20, \, [0, 1] \right)
\end{equation}
where $P_{\text{base}}=0.8$ for canonical motifs (GGACU, GAACU, AGACU, AAACU) and $P_{\text{base}}=0.5$ for variants; $f_{\text{GC}}^{\text{local}}$ is GC fraction in the $\pm$10\,nt context window. This GC-context model reflects the observation that structured regions have higher m6A deposition rates \citep{liu2022}. \textbf{Note:} This is motif-level screening, not site-level prediction validated against m6A-Atlas/RMBase.

\paragraph{m6A effect directionality.}
m6A effect on immunogenicity is context-dependent, not uniformly suppressive. Coding region m6A can lead to YTHDF2-mediated degradation, reducing RNA available for RIG-I recognition \citep{chen2019}. However, IRES-proximal m6A enhances translation via YTHDF1, potentially increasing immune visibility \citep{meyer2015}. m6A-modified RNA fragments from degradation can activate RIG-I \citep{liu2022}. The net effect depends on reader protein context (YTHDF1 vs YTHDF2), cell type, and modification site distribution.

\paragraph{PKR activation threshold and partial dsRNA handling.}
PKR requires $>$33\,bp dsRNA for activation \citep{nallagatla2007}. The PKR score accounts for partial dsRNA structures (bulges, internal loops) by counting only paired bases:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{PKR}} = \min\left( 0.50 \cdot f_{\text{dsRNA}} + 0.25 \cdot \frac{L_{\text{max-dsRNA}} - 33}{1000} + 0.20 \cdot f_{\text{GC}} + 0.05 \cdot (1 - P_{\text{mod}}), \, 1.0 \right)
\end{equation}
where $f_{\text{dsRNA}}$ is dsRNA fraction from structure prediction (range 0--0.5, reflecting that RNA contains A/U/G/C with GC fraction bounded by 0.5), $L_{\text{max-dsRNA}}$ is maximum dsRNA region length, and $P_{\text{mod}}$ is a modification penalty for m6A/$\Psi$/ac4C which reduce PKR activation. Bulges and internal loops reduce effective dsRNA length but do not completely abolish scoring; shorter contiguous paired segments within a disrupted stem contribute proportionally.

\paragraph{Sequence complexity for folding kinetics.}
Sequence complexity $C_{\text{seq}}$ is defined via Shannon entropy of the suboptimal structure probability distribution:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{seq}} = \text{clip}\left( \frac{n_{\text{struct}} - 1}{10} \cdot 0.30 + \frac{\Delta E_{\text{spread}}}{20} \cdot 0.30 + \frac{H_{\text{struct}}}{3} \cdot 0.40, \, [0, 1] \right)
\end{equation}
where $n_{\text{struct}}$ is the number of sampled suboptimal structures (via RNAsubopt within 10\,kcal/mol), $\Delta E_{\text{spread}}$ is the energy range across structures, and $H_{\text{struct}} = -\sum_i p_i \log_2(p_i)$ is the Boltzmann probability distribution entropy with $p_i = \exp(-\Delta G_i / RT)/\sum_j \exp(-\Delta G_j / RT)$ at $RT=0.616$\,kcal/mol (37$^\circ$C). Higher complexity indicates more metastable states and functional potential. When ViennaRNA is unavailable, a fallback estimates $C_{\text{seq}} \approx (1 - f_{\text{GC}}) \times 0.5$.

\subsection*{Four-dimension evaluation}
Dimensions: Clinical (drug, 35\%), Binding (epitope, 25\%), Kinetics (PK, 20\%), circRNA (20\%). Base weights are author-informed heuristics reflecting perceived clinical importance, not empirically calibrated; sensitivity analysis ($\pm$50\% perturbation) shows decisions are stable. Go/Conditional/No-Go thresholds ($\geq$0.65/0.35--0.65/$<$0.35) and safety override (genotoxicity risk $>$0.7 forces No-Go) are heuristic, not derived from retrospective clinical validation; clinical decision-making requires patient-specific factors beyond this tool's scope.

\textbf{Note on Gene Signature dimension:} A gene signature dimension (five-gene signature derived from ADC literature for small-molecule target availability) was initially included with 15\% weight. However, evaluation on TCGA/METABRIC cohorts ($n{=}3\,064$) produced C-index$=$0.52, indicating predictive performance no better than random. This dimension was therefore removed from the evaluation framework prior to manuscript revision (reviewer consensus R1+R4).
