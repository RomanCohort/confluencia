\section{Discussion}

Confluencia evaluates circRNA-specific dimensions absent from existing platforms. DBTL architecture: 4D evaluation (Design), RNACTM configuration (Build), simulation and assays (Test), open training pipelines (Learn; accessible but not yet exercised).

At early-stage candidate screening, accessibility outweighs marginal accuracy gains. A tool 2\% less accurate but runnable locally on every student laptop impacts the field more than one 2\% more accurate behind a GPU paywall, especially for student-driven projects like iGEM where compute budgets are limited. Confluencia embodies this: core evaluation requires no GPU; embeddings are GPU-optional. Three interfaces (GUI IDE, R 27 functions, VS Code 11 commands) share one Python backend, maximizing entry points for diverse users.

\paragraph{Comparison with existing tools.}
Unlike LinearDesign (mRNA codon optimization) and CIRCexplorer (circRNA annotation), Confluencia provides \textbf{prospective} circRNA vaccine design with immunogenicity prediction. On 10 benchmark sequences: Confluencia correctly identified 3 sequences with high inverted repeat potential (RIG-I risk) that LinearDesign scored favorably, demonstrating circRNA-specific risk detection. circInteractome predicts miRNA/RBP interactions but lacks immunogenicity scoring. NetMHCpan excels at MHC binding (AUC 0.92--0.96) but does not assess innate immune pathway activation. Confluencia's circRNA-specific immunogenicity scoring fills this gap, complementing rather than replacing these specialized tools.

\paragraph{Future validation collaboration.}
Direct experimental validation is essential for empirical weight calibration and clinical translation. In collaboration with medical school partners, planned wet-lab validation includes: (1) IFN-$\alpha$/ELISA assays on circRNA sequences spanning predicted immunogenicity range; (2) qRT-PCR validation of RIG-I pathway markers; (3) Half-life measurements via qRT-PCR time course. These experiments will provide ground truth for weight optimization and are expected within the next research cycle. Until then, users should interpret immunogenicity scores as \textbf{qualitative risk indicators}, not validated quantitative predictions.

Three computational validations: (1) immunogenicity direction consistency ($\rho{=}0.93$); (2) 4D decisions stable under ${\pm}$50\% weight perturbation (Kinetics most critical: removal shifts $\Psi$ from Go to Conditional). All are computational; wet-lab data will provide independent benchmarks.

Accuracy trade-offs reflect the accessibility-first design. Drug binding R$^2{=}0.95$ matches literature; efficacy R$^2{=}0.577$ (GroupKFold, primary metric) on 91K data is acceptable for triage, with a 0.17 gap vs random-split (0.742) reflecting scaffold-dependent generalization. Morgan FP removal improves binding 0.67$\to$0.95, validating sample-size-adaptive switching. MHC AUC 0.80 underperforms NetMHCpan (0.92--0.96), but focused-repertoire alleles are comparable; the gap reflects breadth over depth, intentional for broad screening. Importantly, MHC binding prediction (epitope pipeline) addresses adaptive immunity (T-cell antigen presentation), while circRNA immunogenicity scoring addresses innate immunity (RIG-I/TLR/PKR sensing). These are two distinct biological processes that should not be conflated.

Scope clarifications: ADMET endpoints (hERG, AMES, CYP450) apply only to the small-molecule drug payload, not the circRNA vehicle; circRNA-specific toxicity is captured by innate immune pathway scoring. RNACTM is a parameterized dose-response scenario analysis tool, not a clinical trial simulator; rate constants are predominantly from mouse/in~vitro literature and lack human clinical validation. 4D base weights are author-informed heuristics, not clinically calibrated; sensitivity analysis confirms decision stability under $\pm$50\% perturbation.

Limitations: (1) miRNA/RBP/translation lack evaluation ($u{=}0.8$, effective weight ${\sim}$4\%); (2) no inter-individual variability; (3) heuristic immunogenicity weights; (4) DBTL Learn awaits experimental data; (5) RNACTM half-life consistency is by design (17\% expression window deviation is the informative metric); (6) ADMET scope limited to drug payload; (7) MHC binding (adaptive) and circRNA immunogenicity (innate) are distinct but both relevant for safety; (8) RNACTM parameters are predominantly from mouse/in vitro studies; clinical PK prediction requires experimental validation.
