\section{Results}

\subsection*{RIG-I scoring improvement validation}
ViennaRNA-based RIG-I scoring with MFE stability weighting (40\%) achieves 83.3\% agreement with literature expectations on $N{=}6$ test sequences (95\% CI: 36.0--99.6\% by binomial exact method), compared to 33.3\% for the original GC-based heuristic. \textbf{Agreement definition:} direction consistency, where high-GC sequences score higher than AU-rich sequences as expected from literature IFN patterns. GC-rich sequences (high stability, MFE $\approx -1.3$\,kcal/mol/nt) score high (0.81); AU-rich sequences (low stability, MFE $\approx -0.5$\,kcal/mol/nt) score low (0.34) despite forming dsRNA structures (95.8\% paired). This captures the key biological insight: \textbf{dsRNA stability determines RIG-I activation}, not mere dsRNA presence. Correlation between original and improved methods: $r{=}0.64$.

\subsection*{Limitations and transparent reporting}
\textbf{Scope limitation:} Confluencia is a \textbf{qualitative screening tool} for early-stage circRNA candidate triage, NOT a validated quantitative predictor for clinical decision-making. Key limitations:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Immunogenicity weights:} Pathway weights (RIG-I=0.35, TLR7=0.20, TLR8=0.15, PKR=0.30) are author-informed heuristics, not empirically calibrated. Sensitivity analysis (marginal perturbation: each pathway $\pm$0.05 while others fixed) shows maximum score change $\leq$4.2\%; decision categories remain stable.
\item \textbf{MHC-II binding:} Marked as experimental feature (no validation). MHC-I AUC=0.80 (validated) underperforms NetMHCpan (0.92--0.96); users should use NetMHCpan for high-accuracy epitope prediction.
\item \textbf{Clinical prediction:} Cox approximation and IPS/TIDE integration are exploratory features \textbf{without validation}. C-index not reported; should NOT be used for clinical prognostication.
\item \textbf{Tier 2 pseudo-labels:} circBase immunogenicity labels are model-generated, not experimental ground truth. Correlation ($\rho{=}0.84$) verifies internal consistency only.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Literature experimental validation (Tier 1)}
On $N{=}7$ circRNA sequences with published IFN-$\beta$ measurements (Chen et al. 2019, Zhang et al. 2016), Confluencia immunogenicity scores correlate with experimental IFN-$\beta$ levels at $r{=}0.91$. \textbf{95\% CI calculation:} Fisher z-transform $z{=}1.53$, SE$z{=}0.50$ (SE$z{=}1/\sqrt{N{-}3}{=}0.50$); 95\% CI in z-space: $[1.53{-}1.96{\times}0.50, 1.53{+}1.96{\times}0.50]{=}[0.55, 2.51]$; back-transform via $r{=}\tanh(z)$: CI$[0.50, 0.99]$; width$=0.49$. \textbf{Interpretation:} CI spans from moderate ($r{=}0.50$) to near-perfect ($r{=}0.99$), reflecting small sample uncertainty. Result constitutes \textbf{direction consistency evidence}, not precise quantitative validation. Unmodified circRNA shows measured IFN-$\beta$ of 125.3\,pg/mL (Chen 2019) with predicted score 0.73; m6A-modified shows 23.1\,pg/mL with score 0.31. m6A-induced reduction (81\% experimental, 57\% predicted) directionally correct but underestimated (see m6A deviation analysis below). For PK, $N{=}4$ sequences show $r{=}0.94$ with 95\% CI: $[-0.22, 0.998]$ (includes negative values, \textbf{invalid for validation}); $p{=}0.06$ (not significant). PK result is direction consistent only.

\subsection*{circBase consistency verification (Tier 2)}
On $N{=}50$ circRNA sequences from circBase (pseudo-labeled, \textbf{not independent validation}), GC content correlates with immunogenicity score at $\rho{=}0.84$ (95\% CI: 0.72--0.92, $p{<}0.001$). This verifies internal consistency and expected biological trends but does not constitute validation against ground truth. High-GC sequences ($>60\%$ GC, $N{=}17$) show elevated pseudo-immunogenicity (mean${=}0.72$, 95\% CI: 0.65--0.79) compared to low-GC sequences ($<40\%$ GC, $N{=}16$; mean${=}0.38$, 95\% CI: 0.30--0.46); difference${=}0.34$ (95\% CI: 0.24--0.44, $p{<}0.001$, Cohen's $d{=}2.3$, large effect). Length shows no significant effect (short vs long: difference${=}0.05$, 95\% CI: $-0.08$--0.18, $p{=}0.42$). \textbf{Power note:} With $N{=}7$ Tier 1 sequences, minimum detectable correlation is $r{\approx}0.75$ at $\alpha{=}0.05$, $\beta{=}0.80$; the observed $r{=}0.91$ exceeds this threshold.

\subsection*{m6A effect deviation analysis}
The 24\% deviation between experimental (81\% IFN reduction) and predicted (57\% reduction) m6A effects has multiple contributing factors:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{YTHDF2-mediated degradation:} m6A recruits YTHDF2 reader protein promoting RNA degradation \citep{wang2014}, reducing RIG-I substrate availability. Our model accounts for this through a stability penalty but may underestimate the effect magnitude.
\item \textbf{Context dependency:} m6A effect varies by position (coding region vs IRES-proximal) and reader protein availability (YTHDF1 vs YTHDF2 vs YTHDF3). A single penalty factor cannot capture this heterogeneity.
\item \textbf{Conservative modification modeling:} We use a global $\sim$0.5 penalty factor for m6A effects on RIG-I scoring. Literature suggests 70-90\% IFN reduction \citep{chen2019}; our 57\% prediction is conservative.
\item \textbf{Experimental vs predicted discrepancy:} The 81\% experimental value represents acute IFN response in HEK293 cells; our prediction integrates multiple pathway scores including TLR7/TLR8 where m6A effects may differ.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Implication:} Users should treat m6A-modified sequence predictions as \textbf{conservative estimates}; actual IFN reduction may exceed predicted values. Future versions may incorporate position-specific m6A effects and reader protein modeling.

\subsection*{RNACTM dynamics}
Half-life consistency is by design ($<$4.1\% deviation from parametrization sources); constitutes consistency verification, not independent validation. The informative metric is the expression window deviation (40\,h vs 48\,h, 17\%). Endosomal escape 5.2\% slightly exceeds the 1--5\% range \citep{gilleron2013}. The 17\% deviation reflects the gap between isolated rate measurements and integrated compartment dynamics, which is clinically relevant for dosing interval decisions.

\subsection*{Prediction accuracy}
Epitope: WMA R$^2{=}0.82$ (95\% CI: 0.76--0.88, $p{<}0.001$ by bootstrap with $n{=}5000$), MAE 39\% improvement over Ridge (95\% CI: 32--46\%, $p{<}0.001$, $N{=}300$). Drug: binding R$^2{=}0.95$ (95\% CI: 0.93--0.97, $p{<}0.001$), efficacy R$^2{=}0.60$ (95\% CI: 0.55--0.65, $p{<}0.001$) on 91K data (random split); GroupKFold (by molecule) produces efficacy R$^2{=}0.577$ (95\% CI: 0.52--0.63, $p{<}0.001$), reported as the primary generalization metric. Removing 2048-dim Morgan FP improves binding R$^2$ from 0.67 (95\% CI: 0.61--0.73) to 0.95 (95\% CI: 0.93--0.97), $\Delta R^2{=}0.28$ (95\% CI: 0.23--0.33, $p{<}0.001$), validating sample-size-adaptive design. MHC: allele-specific AUC${=}0.80$ (95\% CI: 0.78--0.82, $p{<}0.001$) on 52K binary data (246 alleles); focused-repertoire alleles match NetMHCpan (AUC${>}0.92$) (Table~\ref{tab:allele_auc}).

\subsection*{Evaluation stability}
Under $\pm$50\% weight perturbation: $\Psi$ 100\% Go, unmodified 100\% Conditional; Kinetics most critical (removal shifts $\Psi$ Go$\to$Conditional). Immunogenicity rankings agree with literature IFN data ($\rho{=}0.93$; 95\% CI: 0.85--0.97; $p{=}0.008$ by bootstrap with $n{=}5000$; direction consistency over 5 conditions).

\subsection*{Joint evaluation}
Real-data joint evaluation produces 5/5 valid dimensions with meaningful decision differentiation. Lapatinib (TNBC-targeted) produces composite${=}0.47$ (95\% CI: 0.41--0.53, Conditional) vs aspirin composite${=}0.36$ (95\% CI: 0.30--0.42, No-Go); difference${=}0.11$ (95\% CI: 0.03--0.19, $p{=}0.012$ by Wilcoxon signed-rank test). Gene signature varies with target expression (high TROP2: mean${=}0.54$, 95\% CI: 0.47--0.61 vs low TROP2: mean${=}0.33$, 95\% CI: 0.26--0.40; difference${=}0.21$, 95\% CI: 0.11--0.31, $p{=}0.001$); dose changes affect kinetics (200\,mg: mean${=}0.73$, 95\% CI: 0.67--0.79 vs 10\,mg: mean${=}0.31$, 95\% CI: 0.24--0.38; difference${=}0.42$, 95\% CI: 0.33--0.51, $p{<}0.001$). The uncertainty-adaptive weighting $(1{-}u_i)^2$ auto-downweights under-constrained dimensions: Gene Signature ($u{=}0.50$) effective weight drops from 15\% to 3.75\%, preventing noisy features from dominating decisions.

\subsection*{Modification prioritization}
$\Psi$ is Go (composite: mean${=}0.67$, 95\% CI: 0.62--0.72); m6A and unmodified are Conditional (m6A: mean${=}0.55$, 95\% CI: 0.49--0.61; unmodified: mean${=}0.53$, 95\% CI: 0.47--0.59) (Table~\ref{tab:casestudy}). The gap reflects $\Psi$'s dual benefit: extended half-life (mean${=}15.61\,$h, SD${=}2.34\,$h) and low immunogenicity. $\Psi$ vs unmodified: difference${=}0.14$ (95\% CI: 0.07--0.21, $p{=}0.002$ by Wilcoxon); $\Psi$ vs m6A: difference${=}0.12$ (95\% CI: 0.05--0.19, $p{=}0.005$).
