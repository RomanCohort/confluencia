\section{Discussion}

Confluencia evaluates circRNA-specific dimensions absent from existing platforms. DBTL architecture: 5D evaluation (Design), RNACTM configuration (Build), simulation and assays (Test), open training pipelines (Learn; accessible but not yet exercised).

At early-stage candidate screening, accessibility outweighs marginal accuracy gains. A tool 2\% less accurate but runnable locally on every student laptop impacts the field more than one 2\% more accurate behind a GPU paywall---especially for student-driven projects like iGEM where compute budgets are limited. Confluencia embodies this: core evaluation requires no GPU; embeddings are GPU-optional. Three interfaces (GUI IDE, R 27 functions, VS Code 11 commands) share one Python backend, maximizing entry points for diverse users.

Three computational validations: (1) immunogenicity direction consistency ($\rho{=}0.93$); (2) 5D decisions stable under ${\pm}$50\% weight perturbation (Kinetics most critical: removal shifts $\Psi$ from Go to Conditional); (3) gene signature stratification ($p{<}0.001$). All are computational; wet-lab data will provide independent benchmarks.

Accuracy trade-offs reflect the accessibility-first design. Drug binding R$^2{=}0.95$ matches literature; efficacy R$^2{=}0.60$ on 91K data is acceptable for triage. Morgan FP removal improves binding 0.67$\to$0.95, validating sample-size-adaptive switching. MHC AUC 0.80 underperforms NetMHCpan (0.92--0.96), but focused-repertoire alleles are comparable; the gap reflects breadth over depth, intentional for broad screening.

Limitations: (1) miRNA/RBP/translation lack evaluation ($u{=}0.8$, effective weight ${\sim}$4\%); (2) no inter-individual variability; (3) heuristic immunogenicity weights; (4) DBTL Learn awaits experimental data; (5) RNACTM half-life consistency is by design (17\% expression window deviation is the informative metric).
