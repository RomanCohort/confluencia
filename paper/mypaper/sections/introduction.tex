\section{Introduction}

Therapeutic circRNA offers advantages over linear mRNA in stability, prolonged expression, and tunable immunogenicity \citep{wesselhoeft2018,chen2019}. Yet circRNA drug evaluation requires reasoning across two fundamentally different therapeutic modalities---the small-molecule drug (ADMET, MHC binding) and the circRNA vector (half-life, immunogenicity, TME)---a cross-modality assessment that no existing tool provides. NetMHCpan handles MHC binding \citep{reynisson2020} but ignores circRNA dynamics; ADMETlab covers toxicity \citep{xiong2024} but lacks circRNA-specific PK; population PK software models small molecules but cannot simulate nucleotide modification effects on circRNA half-life ($\Psi$: ${\sim}15$\,h vs unmodified: 6.24\,h). Each tool operates in a single modality silo, producing partial assessments that miss critical interplays---e.g., $\Psi$ extends half-life but raises immunogenicity risk for the co-administered drug.

Confluencia bridges this gap with an architecture that evaluates both modalities and produces cross-modality reasoning: (1) RNACTM, a six-compartment ODE model computing circRNA PK from literature ranges rather than fitted data; (2) a sample-size-adaptive Mixture-of-Experts ensemble \citep{jacobs1991} for small-molecule binding prediction; and (3) an uncertainty-adaptive five-dimension evaluation that auto-downweights under-constrained dimensions, yielding integrated Go/Conditional/No-Go recommendations from both modality assessments simultaneously.
