\section{Introduction}

Therapeutic circular RNA (circRNA) offers advantages over linear mRNA in structural stability, prolonged expression, and tunable immunogenicity \citep{wesselhoeft2018,chen2019}. Yet circRNA drug development faces a fundamental data barrier: unlike small molecules (ChEMBL, $>$2M assays) or linear mRNA (clinical PK datasets from COVID vaccines), circRNA therapeutics have no public pharmacokinetic datasets, no standardized clinical endpoints, and fewer than 300 labeled wet-lab samples in typical studies.

This data vacuum has a direct consequence---no specialized computational tool exists for circRNA drug evaluation. Researchers must cobble together generic predictors: NetMHCpan for MHC binding \citep{reynisson2020}, ADMETlab for toxicity \citep{xiong2024}, and population PK software (Monolix, Phoenix) designed for small molecules---none of which model circRNA-specific dynamics such as nucleotide modification effects on half-life ($\Psi$: ${\sim}15$\,h vs unmodified: 6.24\,h), LNP delivery pharmacokinetics, or the interplay between circRNA immunogenicity and drug ADMET profiles.

Confluencia fills this gap with three design choices specifically motivated by data scarcity: (1) RNACTM, a six-compartment ODE model whose rate constants are computed from literature-reported PK parameter ranges rather than fitted from patient data, enabling 72\,h trajectory simulation for five nucleotide modifications; (2) a sample-size-adaptive Mixture-of-Experts ensemble \citep{jacobs1991} that automatically reduces model complexity when $N<300$; and (3) an uncertainty-adaptive five-dimension evaluation that automatically downweights dimensions with low prediction confidence, preventing overconfident conclusions from under-constrained data. Together, these enable cross-module reasoning---such as ``$\Psi$ extends half-life but raises immunogenicity risk''---that no single specialized tool can produce.
